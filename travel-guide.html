Best Time to Visit Uganda

Uganda is a year-round destination. Unlike some African countries with distinct safari seasons, Uganda's equatorial location means you can travel any month. But some months are better for specific experiences.

☀️ Dry Seasons (Best for Trekking)

Easier hiking trails, better wildlife viewing near water sources, lower malaria risk.

  • June – August: Peak season. Book permits 3–6 months ahead.
  • December – February: Shorter dry window. Great for combining parks.

🌧️ Wet Seasons (Best for Birding & Scenery)

Lush landscapes, fewer tourists, migratory birds present, lower lodge rates.

  • March – May: Long rains. Some roads challenging.
  • September – November: Short rains. Still very travelable.

Our Honest Recommendation

If gorilla trekking is your priority, aim for June–August or December–February. But don't let the rains scare you off — we've run successful treks in April and November. The forest is always lush, gorillas don't care about rain, and you'll have fewer fellow trekkers on the trail. Just bring a good rain jacket.

Destinations

Uganda's National Parks at a Glance

🦍

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Mountain gorilla trekking. Half the world's population lives here. Dense, ancient rainforest. Multiple trailheads.

Best for: Gorilla trekking, birding
Drive from Kampala: 8–10 hours
🌊

Murchison Falls NP

The Nile explodes through a 7-meter gorge. Game drives with elephants, giraffes, lions. Nile boat cruise.

Best for: Classic safari, the waterfall
Drive from Kampala: 5–6 hours
🦁

Queen Elizabeth NP

Tree-climbing lions in Ishasha. Kazinga Channel boat cruise. Over 600 bird species. Crater lakes.

Best for: Wildlife diversity, birds
Drive from Kampala: 6–7 hours
🐵

Kibale Forest

Chimpanzee tracking capital. 13 primate species. Bigodi Wetland for birds and monkeys.

Best for: Chimpanzees, primates
Drive from Kampala: 5–6 hours
🦓

Lake Mburo NP

Uganda's most accessible park. Zebras, giraffes, impala. Boat cruises, walking safaris, horseback riding.

Best for: Short trips, families
Drive from Kampala: 4 hours
🏝️

Lake Bunyonyi

29 islands in a crater lake. Swimming, canoeing, relaxation. Perfect post-trek recovery destination.

Best for: Relaxation, romance
Drive from Kampala: 7–8 hours
Gorilla Trekking

Everything You Need to Know About Gorilla Trekking

The Basics

  • Permit cost: $800 per person (foreign non-resident), $1,500 for habituation experience
  • Minimum age: 15 years. No exceptions. UWA checks passports.
  • Maximum group size: 8 trekkers per gorilla family
  • Time with gorillas: Exactly 1 hour once you reach them
  • Permits available daily: Approximately 150 across Bwindi and Mgahinga

The Trek Itself

Treks range from 1 hour to 6+ hours depending on where the gorilla family is that day. The forest is dense, at altitude, and often muddy. You need reasonable fitness — not marathon training, but the ability to walk uphill on uneven ground for several hours.

Porters are available at the trailhead for about $15. Hire one. They carry your daypack, help you on steep sections, and it's direct community income.

The Rules (Enforced)

  • 7-meter distance from gorillas at all times
  • No flash photography
  • No trekking if you're sick — gorillas have no immunity to human diseases
  • No eating or drinking near the gorillas
  • Keep voices low and movements slow
  • No direct eye contact with silverbacks — they interpret it as a challenge

When to Book Permits

For peak season (June–August, December): book 3–6 months in advance. Permits sell out weeks ahead. For other months: 1–2 months is usually sufficient. We book permits only after receiving your deposit — and we confirm within hours.

Preparation

What to Pack for Uganda

✔️ Essentials

  • • Passport — valid 6+ months beyond travel dates
  • • Yellow fever certificate — required for entry
  • • Travel insurance — with medical evacuation cover
  • • Sturdy hiking boots — waterproof, ankle support
  • • Long trousers — lightweight, quick-dry (2 pairs)
  • • Long-sleeved shirts — for sun and insect protection
  • • Rain jacket — lightweight, packable
  • • Insect repellent — DEET-based

👍 Recommended

  • • Garden gloves — for gripping vegetation while trekking
  • • Gaiters — keep mud and ants out of your boots
  • • Binoculars — for birding and distant wildlife
  • • Power bank — limited charging at some lodges
  • • Reusable water bottle — we provide filtered water
  • • Head torch — for walking around lodges at night
  • • Neutral-coloured clothing — khaki, olive, brown. Avoid bright colours and dark blue (attracts tsetse flies).
Entry Requirements

Visas & Entry Requirements

East Africa Tourist Visa

This is the recommended option if you're visiting Uganda, Kenya, and/or Rwanda on one trip.

  • Cost: $100
  • Validity: 90 days, multiple entry between Uganda, Kenya, and Rwanda
  • Apply: Online at visas.immigration.go.ug or on arrival

Uganda e-Visa (Single Country)

  • Cost: $50 (single entry)
  • Apply: Online before travel. Processing takes 2–3 business days.
  • What you need: Passport copy, yellow fever certificate, travel itinerary, passport photo

Important: Visa policies change. We'll confirm the current requirements when you book with us. Most nationalities can also obtain a visa on arrival at Entebbe Airport for $50 (cash, USD).

Peace of Mind

Safety & Health

Is Uganda Safe for Tourists?

Yes. Uganda is one of the safest countries in East Africa for tourists. The tourism infrastructure is well-established, and Ugandans are genuinely welcoming. Standard precautions apply — don't walk alone at night in unfamiliar urban areas, keep valuables secure, use reputable transport. On safari, you're with a guide at all times.

Health Precautions

  • Yellow fever vaccination: Required for entry. Carry your certificate.
  • Malaria: Present in Uganda. Take prophylaxis, use insect repellent, sleep under nets (provided at all lodges).
  • Travel insurance: Mandatory for all our clients. Must include medical evacuation cover.
  • Drinking water: We provide filtered water. Avoid tap water.
  • COVID-19: Check current requirements before travel. Policies change.
